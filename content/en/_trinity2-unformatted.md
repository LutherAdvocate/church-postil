## Lent

### Marys Annunciation Day; Luk 1: 26-38

#### Luke 1: 26–38

    When Elizabeth was in her sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent by God to a town in Galilee called Nazareth, v27 to a virgin betrothed to a man named Joseph, of the house of David. The virgin’s name was Mary. v28 And the angel came to her and greeted her, saying: “The Lord is with you, O favored one!” v29 She was greatly troubled by these words and wondered what this greeting might mean. v30 Then the angel said to her, “Do not be afraid, Mary! For you have found favor with God. v31 Behold, you will conceive and give birth to a son, and you shall name him Jesus. v32 He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give him the throne of his father David; v33 he will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and his kingdom will have no end.” v34 Mary said to the angel, “How can this be, since I have never been with a man?” v35 The angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore, the child to be born will also be called holy, the Son of God. v36 Even your relative Elizabeth has conceived a son in her old age. She, who was said to be barren, is now in her sixth month; v37 for nothing is impossible with God.” v38 Then Mary said, “Behold, I am the Lord’s servant. Let it be done to me according to your word.” Then the angel left her.

#### Exposition of the Gospel

##### Introduction

1.According to the promise, Christ was to be born of the house of David. That is why all of this is emphasized here at the beginning of the Gospel.

2.The throne of David, his father, is the kingdom of Christ. But in order to understand what kind of kingdom this is, we must recall here the words of Isaiah in chapter 9, which are suﬃciently explained in the epistle for Christmas Day, and also this word from Jeremiah: “In his days Judah will be saved, and Israel will dwell in safety. He shall be called ‘The Lord Is Our Righteousness.’ Jer. 23:6. That is to say, through Christ we are safe, even though we are sinners.

3.Everything that comes from carnal conception is unclean; therefore, Christ was born of a virgin.

This is a joyful and delightful Gospel, in which faith is once again presented to us, just as in all the other Gospels. For we are to hear that the Virgin Mary possessed such faith that we have rarely found its equal in Scripture. Through this, we are also to be encouraged in faith, for this Gospel oﬀers us a lesson and an exhortation.
Here we will see how much faith is at stake, what its nature and essence are, and how it is built upon and relies on nothing other than God’s Word alone. We will observe all of this as we carefully consider the circumstances of this account.

#### The First Lesson

On Faith and Its Struggle, Strength, and Power

First, Mary hears here—and it is announced to her by the angel—that she is to give birth to a child and become a mother. This was no great matter, for any other virgin could easily believe it as well, if she were betrothed to a man. But let us now consider that this virgin herself responds in this way to the angel’s message, saying, “How can this be? I have never been with a man.” She is thus herself astonished by what was proclaimed to her—that she would become a mother, even though she has never been with a man. Yet she believes the angel’s words.

This is a lofty, glorious faith—to become a mother and yet remain a pure virgin. It truly surpasses understanding, thought, and all human reason and experience.

Here, Mary has no example among all creatures on earth to which she can turn for support and strength. Indeed, they are all completely contrary to her faith, for she is the only one who exists who, contrary to all human reason, mind, and thoughts, must give birth and become a mother without the involvement of any man. Do you not think that this scandal to the faith has touched her heart? For she, too, was ﬂesh and blood, just like one of us. Therefore, she had to let go of everything, even herself, and cling solely to the word that the angel proclaimed to her from God.

Second, there is the grandeur of this message, which also challenged her faith. It was announced to her that she would conceive and give birth to the Most High and the Son of God, whose kingdom will have no end. How did that ﬁt with her station in life? She was a poor, lowly girl, perhaps thirteen, fourteen, or ﬁfteen years old. Most likely, she was not particularly well-regarded in Nazareth, as she says of herself in her song of praise: “He has looked upon his lowly servant. ” Her parents were of humble station and status, ordinary people; indeed, who knows if her father and mother were even still alive? Perhaps she was an orphan. And it is not the case, as some have claimed, that her parents, Joachim and Anna, were wealthy and divided their estate into three parts—one part for the churches, another for the poor, and the third left to Mary. These are fabrications, just like so much else that has been told outside of Scripture and without foundation.

Just as it has been with Mary and her faith, so it is with all of us: we must believe what goes against our feelings, thoughts, and experience, and what has no precedent. For this is the nature and character of faith—that it cannot tolerate anything alongside itself that a person might rely on or rest upon, apart from the mere Word of God or the divine promise. Do you not think that the Devil, especially in the ﬁnal hour and when we are in the agony of death, will confront us and say: “Look how the whole world has been forsaken by God and is barely being held together.” Do you think he will do something special for you above all others and save you in this way without any merit on your part? And whatever words he might use, he will present them in the most terrifying way possible.

Mary, too, must have thought this way here and, using her reason, spoken to herself: ‘What new thing is about to happen to you now?’ If she had followed reason, it would certainly have been hard for her; for she had no small reason to do so, since her entire nature and all creation were opposed to it. But… what does she do? She believes, closing her eyes to it, even though reason and all creation are against it. Her heart clings solely to the Word.

That is why the Devil uses every trick in the book and comes up with natural things and stories that go against the word of faith, so that he can lead people away from the word. And if we begin to waver, the Devil has already won. Anyone who struggles with faith will soon encounter things that go against the word. But Faith lets go of all creatures and visible things in the world, even itself, and clings to God’s Word. Yes, that is how it must be, dear friend; faith seeks no other foothold on which to stand. This is also what Christ means when He says in John 8:51 : “Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever keeps My word will never see death.” That is to say, whoever clings ﬁrmly to My promise in faith will not die. For the Lord is not speaking here of keeping God’s commandments, as the philosophical theologians interpret it, but of the words of faith and the Gospel. For in death you will see Hell, the Devil, sin, and your own nature before you—and all that is dreadful. Then you will want to stay here longer, and you will be horriﬁed by this sight. If you remain standing before this sight, you will perish; but if you cling to God’s Word and close your eyes to it, you will stand ﬁrm. How can this be? It is because God’s Word is alive, and death cannot destroy it. Where the Word endures, you will endure as well.

Therefore, it was truly a great faith on the part of the young girl Mary that she overcame all visible, contradictory things and clung solely to the angel’s words. Nor, as I said, did she allow herself to be shaken by the fact that such great things were proclaimed to her—that she was to conceive and give birth to the Son of God and a king. Here, too, she could have said: “Who am I, a poor, little worm, that I should give birth to such a king?” In that way, she would have fallen into doubt. But she closes her eyes to all that, trusting in God that He is mighty enough to bring all these things to fulﬁllment, even though reason and all creation are opposed to them. Because she now believes, what the angel said comes to pass for her.

So it is with us as well, and especially so at the hour of death. Then our nature and the Devil will hold before us the greatness of God’s overwhelming gifts, with which He has abundantly pardoned and showered us—so abundantly that no human heart can comprehend or measure them. Much less can anyone express them in words; for it is an utterly indescribable thing that the one who believes in Christ is a master over death, sin, the Devil, and Hell, and furthermore a co-heir with Christ. A person must be awestruck by this and think thus: You are a poor, wretched human being—who are you that you should live eternally in Heaven and receive these great gifts? You are not ﬁt to share in such great blessings. Then the heart must falter, for it is too small, and the gifts are too great. It cannot comprehend them.

Therefore, one must be wise here and not let oneself be ﬁlled with fear, not look at our worthiness or unworthiness, but at God’s word and promise. It is not our own strength that accomplishes this, but it is God’s good pleasure that allows this to be proclaimed and promised to us.

This is how Christ comforts his disciples in Luke 1 2:32, where he says: “Do not be afraid, little ﬂock, for your Father has chosen to give you the Kingdom.” As if to say: You have not earned it; indeed, you have deserved Hell; but what happens to you is pure grace, promised to you by God’s good pleasure. Therefore, just believe it, and you will have it for certain. It is indeed a great thing that we are God’s children and Christ’s brothers, that we have power over and are masters of death, sin, the Devil, and Hell. But not all people have this power—only those who believe. For whoever believes that God is our Father and we are His children cannot fear anyone, for God is a protector in whose power all things are, and the hearts of all people are in His hand. Let me see who can harm me when I believe this and have this trust in God. Yes, I must carry such faith with me and feel it in my heart, for it will not merely ﬂow from my tongue or linger in my ears, but it penetrates deep into the heart.

But those who are greedy and scrape and tear the goods of this world to themselves, even to the detriment of others, do not have faith. For if they had faith, they would trust God so much that He would provide for them in this life. Since they now have no trust in Him regarding the temporal things with which they must make do here on earth, how then can they believe that He will eternally nourish and sustain them? This food here is temporal and perishable, but the food the soul has is eternal, and without that food the soul cannot live either, just as the body cannot live without physical food. Therefore, when the soul is deprived of spiritual food, it is already dead and has no life within itself, which is given to it solely through Christ. As He Himself says in John 1 1 :25–26: “I am the resurrection and the life; whoever believes in Me will live, even if he dies. And everyone who lives and believes in Me will never die for all eternity.”

The same is true of our enemies; when I trust in God—that He is almighty and has all things in His power—I do not fear my enemy; for God holds my enemy’s heart and thoughts just as ﬁrmly in His hand as He does mine. He is Lord over life and death, let alone over human beings. This is what Solomon means when he says in Proverbs 1 5:1 1 : “The realm of the dead and the underworld are open to the Lord; how much more so the hearts of men!” Since death and life are in His hand, who then can harm me against His will? If, however, he should allow me to be killed, well then, let his will be done; he can surely bring me back to life whenever he chooses. We have all this through Christ when we believe in him. Therefore, we must pay close attention here to the angel’s words when he says that Christ is to have a kingdom and reign forever. For the greater we can make this King, the more our faith is strengthened, if we believe that He is mighty, just as the angel says of Him. If we believe this, then we have nothing to fear; we need not fear death, nor Hell, nor even any human being.

The three young men in Daniel chapter 4 had such faith when they allowed themselves to be bound and thrown into a blazing furnace; for they had such conﬁdence in God that He could save them even from the midst of the ﬁre, which indeed happened. This is also what the psalmist means when he says: “Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear “I shall fear no evil, for You are with me; Your rod and Your staﬀ, they comfort me.”Ps. 23:4. And in Ps. 3:7–8 , David says: “I will not fear ten thousand warriors who besiege me on every side. Arise, O Lord; save me, my God! For you have struck my enemies on the cheek and shattered the teeth of the wicked.”And Paul says in Romans 1 4:7–8: “For none of us lives to himself, and none of us dies to himself; for if we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord. So whether we live or die, we belong to the Lord.”If anyone now does me harm, he does not do it to me, but to my Lord. No one can have this comforting conﬁdence unless he has a strong faith directed solely toward God and His Word. The human heart cannot fathom such lofty blessings; indeed, it is horriﬁed by them where faith is lacking.

Therefore, this Gospel presents us with an example of great faith, so that we may be admonished and encouraged to believe, and thus continually grow and be strengthened in the faith. Do you think that Mary felt no shock in her heart? Was she to have believed these lofty things immediately and without hesitation? No, she certainly felt a pang of unbelief within herself; otherwise, she would not have asked and said, “How can this be? I have never been with a man.” That is why the angel also treats her with great gentleness, speaks kindly, strengthens her, and says: “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.

Therefore, the child to be born will also be called holy, the Son ofGod.” As if to say: “What I am telling you is not the work of human hands; God will supernaturally work His deed within you, which neither you nor I can understand; therefore, believe my words and give glory to God. What seems impossible to you is entirely possible for God.” He continues to comfort and strengthen her with an example, saying:


v36–37 Even your relative Elizabeth has conceived a son, now in her old age. She, of whom it is said that she is barren, is in her sixth month; for nothing is impossible with God With this example, the angel wants to show her that even things that are impossible by nature are possible for God. Although Elizabeth is old and barren—and has become the talk of the town, so that people called her “the barren one”—she has nevertheless become pregnant by her husband Zechariah and will give birth to a child. Therefore, let this example convince you, and believe my word: it will truly happen to you just as I have announced to you.
These words spoken by the angel— “Nothing is impossible with God”—should also comfort us when we waver and tremble in our faith, just as Christ also says in Mark 9:23: “Everything is possible for one who believes.” For Christ knows well that we cannot comprehend the overﬂowing riches of His grace, for they exceed all our understanding and thought; therefore, He urges us only to faith, for which nothing is too diﬃcult.

Thus, we have brieﬂy reviewed the main passages of this Gospel, from which we learn what true and right faith is like and what its essence is—namely, that it clings and holds fast, not to any external thing or any deed, whatever it may be called, but solely to the Word of God. Now we will also brieﬂy consider the spiritual meaning or secret interpretation, if we have enough time left.


#### The Symbolic Meaning

First, we are reminded of how a true preacher must be equipped if he is to preach God’s Word fruitfully, for Luke writes that the angel was sent by God. This tells us, ﬁrst of all, that a preacher must be sent by God and must not preach his own words but God’s Word, and that he must not be the master of the Word but its messenger and servant. For “angel” is a Greek word meaning “messenger, ” and it refers to a messenger who carries the message in his mouth—not in letters, but in words. As Paul says in 1 Corinthians 4:1 : “This is how one should regard us: as servants ofChrist and stewards ofthe mysteries ofGod.” We also read in Luke 9:52 that when the Lord was going up to Jerusalem, He sent a messenger ahead of Him to one of the Samaritan towns to make arrangements for lodging. Here, too, the text uses the word “angel.” However, it was certainly not an angel, but his disciples. That is why I have translated it as “messenger,” not “angel.”

From this we must now learn that no one should preach unless he has been sent there by God—that is, unless he has a sure sign from God or has been called to do so by the proper authority—so that he may hold his own against the false apostles who lack this. And that is why Paul so highly and frequently, in almost all his letters, extols his apostolic oﬃce when he says: “Paul, a servant ofJesus Christ, called to be an apostle, set apart to proclaim the gospel ofGod.” When a worldly-wise person comes along and seeks to judge this matter by reason, he will surely say that Paul was a proud man who merely boasts of himself, yet his praise seeks nothing else but to testify that his word is the word of God—in contrast to the false apostles, of whom there were many in his day who could not boast of their calling or prove that they truly possessed the word of God.

Next, he calls upon the angel Gabriel, whose name means “God’s strength”—one who has been made strong and powerful by God. For when a preacher does not draw his strength and power from God, he will not continue to preach for very long or proclaim the truth with perseverance, but he will withdraw when persecution and death draw near. He cannot endure, for he does not have any strength; his name is not Gabriel—that is to say, he has not been made strong by God. Therefore, all preachers must be called Gabriel—that is to say, they must possess and feel God’s strength within them. Then they will proclaim God’s Word with fruitfulness and perseverance, for then fruitfulness is sure to follow.
Where a preacher lacks such strength from God, he should remain silent; he will accomplish little, indeed, absolutely nothing. God’s Word is not such a trivial thing that it gains entry only with fear and trembling.

The devil, the world, and everything in this world that is ﬁne, great, and glorious will oppose it. Therefore, there must also be great strength at hand to ﬁght against it.

Human strength certainly cannot do this. God’s power and might must do it; otherwise, nothing will come of it.

This has been said about the name Gabriel. It follows that this angel Gabriel was sent to the town of Nazareth, to the Virgin Mary, who was betrothed to a man named Joseph. But Mary is actually a Hebrew name meaning a drop of water that remains hanging in a bucket or a jar. This tiny drop bears no comparison to the sea or a great lake. This symbolizes the Jewish people, to whom God’s word—the Holy Gospel—was sent, though only the smallest group, represented here by Mary, accepts and believes it. For, with the exception of the apostles, there were only a few Jews who accepted the Gospel. What are 72 people compared to a people as exceedingly numerous as the Jews?

But the fact that this Virgin Mary was betrothed to a man and yet remains a virgin means that the Gospel is preached only to those who are oppressed and tormented under the Law. For to be under the Law is like being subject to a husband. As long as the husband lives, the wife is subject to him. In the same way, the Law oppresses and torments as long as it is in eﬀect, but just as the wife is free when her husband dies, so too is the Law abolished and can never again oppress, drive, demand, torment, or aﬄict. Paul beautifully explains this in Romans 7:1 –4, where he says: “Do you not know, brothers—I am speaking to people who know the law—that the law has authority over a person only as long as he lives? A married woman is bound by law to her husband as long as he lives; but if he dies, she is released from the law that bound her to him. Therefore, if she marries another while her husband is still alive, she is an adulteress; but if her husband dies, she is free from the law, so that she is not an adulteress if she marries another. So you also, my brothers, have been made dead to the law through the body ofChrist, so that you may belong to another—to him who was raised from the dead—and bear fruit for God.”
The law is the husband, and conscience is the wife. Now the wife must have a husband and yet not have a husband; she must be undeﬁled and remain a virgin for him. This is how it works: We are under the law, and yet not under the law; that is to say, although we live under the law, we are nevertheless not subject to the law, but remain free and unharmed by it, just as Mary here continues to be a virgin with Joseph. But this happens when I live under the law in faith and perform the works of the law freely and willingly, not because of the compulsion of the commandment or in such a way that I believe I will be saved by my works. I cling solely to faith; in it I have enough, I am rich and blessed, but I practice and perform the works—even if there were no law—with a cheerful, joyful, and willing heart, everything that the law alone can demand of me. I could not do this before, when I did not have faith. But I do not rely on that, nor do I rely on my works; rather, my life ﬂows solely from faith.

This is how I now live under the law and yet am not under the law. For I do not perform works because the law demands them, but I am free from the law through faith, which is not bound by any law or work. Yet faith is not idle; rather, it ﬁrst produces true and good works. Yet it does not place its trust in works, but solely in God’s grace. This is what Paul also says in Galatians 2:20: “I no longer live, but Christ lives in me.” As if to say: The works that I do, insofar as I live in the ﬂesh, are not my life—upon which I stand or build—but faith is my comfort, hope, and trust; by faith alone, and not by my works which I perform in the ﬂesh, do I hope to be saved. That is why he also quotes the prophet Habakkuk in Romans 1 :1 7: “The righteous shall live by faith.” And thus faith keeps us pure and blameless before the law.

To help you understand this better, let us consider an example and a parable: If I observe this feast day as if it were commanded of me, and believe that I have done a good deed by keeping it, but sin if I do not keep it holy—if I think this way, then my virginity is already gone. I am deﬁled by the law and have slept with a man. But if I observe the day without thinking that I am thereby doing a particularly good deed, or if I fail to observe it without thinking that I have thereby broken the law—then that is something else. If I do not insist that by observing it I have done a good deed by which I am saved, then I remain pure and uncontaminated by the man. True faith preserves my virginity, but the abuse—that when I did not observe it, I believed I had committed a sin—makes me impure.

Here we see what true faith is, which is not based on any work at all, but solely on God’s sheer, undeserved mercy. But only a few possess such faith. Therefore, Mary here represents a drop in the ocean; for just as the ocean far surpasses a drop of water, so too are true Christians who possess this righteous faith in no way comparable to the vast majority, even though they bear the name.

So now you have heard that the law is the man and conscience is the woman. When conscience clings to the law, so that it commits sin when it does not keep the law and turns a good deed into salvation when it keeps the law, then virginity is already lost. For it is only spiritual virginity—which is founded on faith in Christ and fulﬁlls the law freely, without compulsion, out of love—that pleases God. But when we keep the law or God’s commandments by our natural ability—that is, without the Spirit of God—it results in nothing but hypocrisy and a woman who has many children. For just as a woman bears children with a man, so too does the people under the law: it bears much fruit—that is, it performs many works of the law.

This is also symbolized by Elkanah’s two wives in 1 Samuel 1. One wife, Peninnah, had many children, but the other, Hannah, had none. These are the two kinds of people under the Law. One group performs many works, but without faith, without desire, and reluctantly—and they are like deﬁled women. The other group performs the works of the law in faith, freely, willingly, and with desire. They are pure virgins. The same is true of the pope’s commands. When the pope commands this or that, and I say, “Very well! I will do this deed to please the pope,” but I do not place much emphasis on it, my salvation does not depend on it, and I do it freely—not as if I were compelled to do it—then the bride is still pure and unblemished. But if I think this way: “If you do not keep the Pope’s command, you commit a mortal sin”—then the bride becomes a harlot and loses her virginity. Then she has lost her faith and believes that works make her blessed or damned, and not mere faith in Christ.

Therefore, as I have said, you must also take note that the Gospel must ﬁrst and foremost be preached to those people who are under the Law but do not practice the works of the Law. That is to say, the Gospel is preached to those who feel the weight and burden of the Law—that it demands things of them which they are unable to do by their own strength. They do not approach the Law relying on their natural ability, so that they might keep it solely through outward works; rather, in their anxiety and helplessness, they expect greater help so that they may fulﬁll it from the heart. To such anxious consciences—which acknowledge their wretchedness and sin and are thirsty
—the angel, the preacher of God’s Word, is sent. He proclaims to them the word that they are to become mothers of Christ, in whom God dwells through Christ, God and man.

This must suﬃce regarding this Gospel. We will now add the interpretation of the angel’s greeting.

The Interpretation of the Angel’s Greeting: Hail Mary (Hail, Mary)
It should be noted here that no one should place their trust and hope in the Mother of God or in her merits, for such trust belongs solely to God, as the one and only great object of worship. One may, however, praise and thank God for her and for the grace that has been given to her. One must not love and praise her for any other reason than that she has received such blessings from God by pure grace, without merit—as she herself confesses in her hymn of praise.

Just as when I, by contemplating the heavens, the sun, and all creatures, am moved to praise their Creator and include them in my prayer and praise, saying: “O God! You who have created such lovely and beautiful creatures, grant me true understanding and a strong, ﬁrm faith,” etc. In the same way, you can also include the Mother of God in your prayer here and say: “O God! What a noble human being You have created here; blessed be she; and You, who have honored her so highly, grant me this or that as well.” Whatever you then ask of God, do not let your heart linger with her, but through her press on toward Christ and toward God Himself. That is why the Hail Mary is also worded in such a way as to give God the glory for all things, for it says:

#### Hail, Mary, full of grace

> Hail, Mary, full of grace. The Lord is with you. Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb, Jesus Christ. Amen.1

Here you see that there is no prayer contained within it, but only praise and gloriﬁcation; just as there is no prayer in the ﬁrst words of the Lord’s Prayer, but rather praise and gloriﬁcation of God for being our Father in Heaven. Therefore, we should neither oﬀer a prayer nor an invocation of the Hail Mary, for it is not ﬁtting for us to interpret the words beyond what they say and how the Holy Spirit has placed them. However, they can be understood in two ways. First, as a narrative, in which we recount the grace that God has given her. Second, we may add to this a wish that she be recognized and honored by all.

First, she is full of God’s grace, so she is recognized as being free from all sin, which is an exceedingly great thing. God’s grace ﬁlls her with all that is good and frees her from all that is evil. Second, God is with her; that is to say, all her actions and deeds are godly and take place within her through God’s help. Furthermore, He protects and preserves her from everything that might harm her. Third, she is blessed above all women, not only because she gave birth without labor pains or suﬀering and without harm, surpassing Eve and all other women, but also because she conceived and became pregnant without any sin, through the Holy Spirit—a grace not granted to any other woman. Fourth, her oﬀspring has been blessed—that is, spared the curse that falls upon all of Eve’s children, so that they are conceived in sin and born guilty of death and condemnation—whereas this fruit of her womb alone is blessed, and through it we have all been blessed.

Furthermore, we must now add a prayer or a wish: that we pray for all those who curse this fruit and its mother. But who curses this fruit? All those who persecute and curse His Word, the Gospel, and the faith—as the Jews and the Papists do today. It follows, then, that no one now curses this mother and her fruit as much as those who bless her with many rosaries and always have the Hail Mary on their lips. These are, for the most part, the very ones who curse Christ’s words and the faith most vehemently.

Take note, therefore, that this mother and her fruit are blessed in two ways, physically and spiritually. Physically, with the mouth and with the words of the Hail Mary. They are the worst blasphemers and cursers. Spiritually, with the heart, as I praise and bless her child, Christ, in all his words, deeds, and suﬀerings—which no one does except one who believes in a truly Christian way. For without such faith, no heart is good, but by nature it is full of cursing and blasphemy against God and all His saints. Therefore, it is advisable for one who does not believe to abandon the Hail Mary and all prayer. It is written of such a person that even his prayer becomes sin. May God preserve us from that!

The angel came to her and greeted her with the words: “The Lord is with you, O favored one!” (v. 26).

Elizabeth was ﬁlled with the Holy Spirit and cried out in a loud voice: “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit ofyour womb!” (v. 42).

The original Greek text reads as follows: Θεοτόκε Παρθένε, χαῖρε, κεχαριτωμένη Μαρία, ὁ Κύριος μετὰ σοῦ. Εὐλογημένη, σὺ ἐν γυναιξί, καὶ εὐλογημένος ὁ καρπὸς τῆς κοιλίας σου, ὅτι Σωτήρα ἔτεκες τῶν ψυχῶν ἡμῶν.

Ave María, grátia plena, Dóminus tecum.

Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb, Jesus.
